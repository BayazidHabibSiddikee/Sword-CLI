Objectives
• To understand the working principle of a single acting pneumatic cylinder.

• To design and implement electro-pneumatic circuits controlled by logic gates.

• To demonstrate logical decision-making in practical automation systems.
Theory
A single acting pneumatic cylinder is a linear actuator that uses compressed air to move a piston
in one direction—typically to extend. A spring mechanism inside the cylinder returns the piston to
its original position once the air is released. These cylinders usually operate at an air pressure of
80–100 PSI and are commonly controlled with a 3/2-way valve [1].
When compressed air enters the inlet, it pushes the piston forward. When the air is released and
vented, the spring retracts the piston. These cylinders are cost-effective, air-efficient, and ideal for
applications such as clamping, ejecting, or lifting lightweight objects. However, they generate
force in only one direction and are limited by the return spring strength [2].
In pneumatic systems, logical functions can be replicated using air flow and control valves. High
air pressure (flow) represents a logic 1. No air flow (low pressure) represents a logic 0.
AND Gate (Series Connection)
• Pneumatic Representation: Two 3/2 valves connected in series. Air passes only if both
valves are open.
• Logical Behavior: Output is 1 only if both inputs are 1.

Applications
• Industrial Automation: For logic-driven machine actions
• Safety Systems: Ensuring safe operations using NOR/NAND logic
• Material Handling: Decision-based mechanisms using XOR/XNOR
• Educational Purposes: Understanding practical digital logic
• Simple Automation: Acts as a substitute for PLCs in budget-sensitive setups

Discussion
The experiment effectively demonstrated how logic gates can be used to control pneumatic
actuators. Each logic condition was implemented and observed using the De Lorenzo trainer.
Complex gates like XOR and XNOR showcased real-world control logic. Challenges such as
circuit noise and timing delays were addressed, offering hands-on insight into digital control
systems.
Conclusion
This experiment successfully showcased the integration of digital logic gates with a pneumatic
actuator. By constructing various logic circuits, we were able to observe and understand the
specific conditions required to control the motion of a single acting cylinder. These principles are
foundational to automation, robotics, and industrial control systems.
