Experiment Name: Study the Role of Power Electronics for Environment and Sustainability
Abstract
Power electronics, the branch of electrical engineering concerned with the conversion and
control of electric power using semiconductor switching devices, has become a cornerstone
technology for achieving global energy and environmental sustainability. This report studies the
role that power electronic converters play in enabling renewable energy integration, smart grid
operation, electric transportation, and industrial energy efficiency, thereby reducing greenhouse
gas emissions and supporting a cleaner energy future. The discussion combines general concepts
with country-specific data for Bangladesh, drawing on figures published by the International
Energy Agency (IEA), the Bangladesh Power Development Board (BPDB), and Power Grid
Bangladesh PLC (PGCB), to illustrate the present state of the power sector, the extent of
renewable energy penetration, and the challenges that remain.
Introduction
Power electronics is the application of electronics to the control and conversion of electric power.
Early high-power conversion relied on mercury-arc valves; modern systems instead use
semiconductor switching devices such as diodes, thyristors, power MOSFETs, and IGBTs, with
variable-speed drive systems ranging in power from a few hundred watts to tens of megawatts.
Because almost every stage of modern power generation, transmission, and consumption now
passes through some form of power-electronic converter, the technology sits at the center of the
transition toward a more sustainable energy system.
Energy sustainability refers to meeting present and future energy demand without exhausting or
depleting natural resources, primarily through sources such as solar, wind, geothermal,
hydropower, and ocean energy that are generally available at no fuel cost and can be used
repeatedly. Environmental sustainability, as defined by the United Nations World Commission on
Environment and Development, means acting in a way that ensures future generations retain access
to the natural resources needed for a quality of life at least equal to that of the present generation ,
while improving human welfare within the carrying capacity of the Earth's supporting ecosystems.
Key Features of Power Electronics for Sustainability
Power electronic converters act as the interface that transfers electricity from one stage of the
supply chain to the next, and this flexibility is what allows several key sustainability outcomes to
be achieved:
1. Renewable energy integration: power converters (rectifiers, inverters, DC-DC converters) are the
key technology that allows variable-output sources such as solar photovoltaic and wind turbines to
be connected safely to the grid.
2. Smart grid enablement: power electronic devices give grid operators far more flexible control over
voltage, frequency, and power flow, essential for integrating distributed and intermittent renewable
generation.

3. Clean energy conversion and green industry: high-efficiency converters minimize conversion losses,
and variable-frequency drives cut energy waste in industrial motors, pumps, and compressors.
4. Electric/hybrid vehicles and electric aircraft: battery management systems, chargers, and traction
inverters built on power electronics make electrified transport viable.

Future Prospect
The role of power electronics in the energy sector is expected to expand considerably over the
coming decade. Wide-bandgap semiconductors such as silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride
(GaN) are beginning to replace conventional silicon devices in high-power converters, offering
lower switching losses and higher efficiency. Grid-forming inverters are emerging as a key enabler
for power systems with very high shares of renewable generation. Other significant trends include
battery energy storage paired with power-electronic converters, electric-vehicle charging
infrastructure, DC microgrids for rural electrification, and power electronics in green hydrogen
production through electrolysis.

These figures illustrate why power electronics is particularly important for Bangladesh. Efficient
AC-DC-AC conversion in HVDC interconnectors (such as the Bheramara link with India) allows
cross-border power imports to help cover shortfalls; power-electronic inverters are essential to the
growing installed solar capacity already feeding the grid; and modern variable-frequency drives in
industrial and irrigation loads can materially reduce the demand that must be met from scarce gas-
fired capacity. As Bangladesh's renewable energy targets and planned solar parks come online,
and as the national grid modernises toward smart-grid operation, power electronic converters will
play an increasingly central role in closing the demand-supply gap sustainably rather than through
further fossil-fuel capacity additions.
Conclusion
Power electronics underpins nearly every pathway toward a more sustainable energy system, from
grid-scale renewable integration and smart-grid control to electric transport and industrial
efficiency. Bangladesh-specific data show that demand continues to rise, fossil fuels still dominate
generation, and renewable energy, while growing, remains a modest share of the total mix. Where
natural gas supplies almost half of total energy and evening-peak load-shedding still reaches over
2,500 MW, wider deployment of efficient power-electronic converters, together with continued
investment in solar, wind, and grid-interconnection capacity, offers one of the most practical routes
to closing the demand-supply gap sustainably. Continued research, policy support, and
infrastructure investment will be essential for achieving both energy security and environmental
sustainability in the years ahead.
