Theory

A double-acting hydraulic cylinder is a mechanical actuator that uses pressurized hydraulic fluid
to  generate  linear  motion  and  force  during  both  extension  and  retraction.  In  contrast  to  single-
acting cylinders, which exert force in only one direction and depend on external forces (such as
springs or gravity) for the return stroke, double-acting cylinders apply hydraulic pressure to both
sides of the piston, enabling controlled movement in both directions [1].

Fig.5.1. Double Acting Cylinder

Components

•  Cylinder Barrel: A tube-like structure that houses the piston and hydraulic fluid.
•  Piston:  A  disc  inside  the  barrel  that  separates  it  into  two  chambers  and  moves  when

hydraulic pressure is applied.

•  Piston Rod: A rod connected to the piston that extends through one end of the cylinder to

transfer force to external components.

•  Seals: Prevent hydraulic fluid leakage and help maintain internal pressure.
•  Hydraulic Ports: Two ports positioned at either end of the cylinder for fluid entry and exit

Circuit Buildup

Condition 01: Unstable Electro-drive with Unstable Control

Electro-drive: A push button is used to trigger the solenoid. Since the push button returns to its
original state when released, the electro-drive remains unstable.

Control: A spring return is installed on one side of the directional control valve (DCV), ensuring
the valve automatically returns to its default position when not actuated.

Setup:
•
•
•

Connect the push button to the solenoid valve.
Set up the DCV with a spring return on one side.
Link the DCV to the double-acting hydraulic cylinder.

Applications

Electro-pneumatic  systems  using  double-acting  cylinders  are  extensively  applied  in  industrial
automation, where stable control is crucial for dependable operation. Major applications include:

1.  Packaging Machinery: Power sealing, cutting, and pressing actions, requiring consistent

operation to maintain product quality.

2.  Robotics: Drive robotic grippers and actuators, where stability ensures accurate pick-and-

place tasks.

3.  Automotive Manufacturing: Support assembly and testing operations where precision and

safety are essential.

Discussion

This experiment evaluated the behavior of a double-acting cylinder under four different conditions.
In  Condition  01  (unstable  electro-drive  and  control),  cylinder  motion  was  inconsistent  due  to
unreliable actuation and valve resetting. Condition 02 (unstable electro-drive with stable control)
showed better movement, though minor inconsistencies remained. In Condition 03 (stable electro-
drive  with  unstable  control),  erratic  behavior  persisted  as  the  spring  return  disrupted  valve
operation. Finally, Condition 04 (stable electro-drive and control) achieved smooth, predictable
cylinder motion. The results confirmed that consistent electro-drive and valve control are vital for
reliable electro-pneumatic system performance, matching theoretical expectations.

Conclusion

The experiment successfully demonstrated the impact of stable and unstable controls on a double-
acting cylinder’s performance. Smooth and predictable operation, as seen in Condition 04, required
both stable electro-drive and control. In contrast, Conditions 01 and 03 showed erratic movement
under unstable setups, while Condition 02 offered partial improvement. These findings emphasize
the importance of consistent actuation and valve control for designing reliable electro-pneumatic
systems in industrial automation.
