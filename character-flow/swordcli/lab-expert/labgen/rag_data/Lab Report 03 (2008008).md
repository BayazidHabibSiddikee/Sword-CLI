Experiment No.: 03

Experiment Name:
Simulation and Operation of Pneumatic Circuits for Latching, Timers (On Delay & Off Delay),
Logic Sequencing (A+B+A−B− and A+A−B+B−), and Counter Operations using FluidSIM.

Objectives

•  To simulate and analyze latching circuits using FluidSIM, exploring their functionality and

applications in pneumatic systems.

•  To study and understand the working  principles  of On Delay and Off Delay pneumatic

timers, and their impact on system operation.

•  To implement and observe the behavior of A+B+A−B− and A+A−B+B− logic sequencing

circuits in pneumatic systems, demonstrating their sequence control.

•  To demonstrate and investigate counter operations within pneumatic systems, highlighting

their use in various automated processes.

Theory

In  pneumatics,  various  components  are  used  to  control  the  sequence  and  timing  of  actuator
operations.

Latching Circuit: Latching was used to maintain the actuator’s state even after the control signal
was removed. A self-holding circuit was built using a push-button and a directional control valve.

On Delay Timer (Normally Closed Time Delay Valve): An On Delay Timer was used to delay
the extension of an actuator after a signal was given. A normally closed (NC) time delay valve was
used for this purpose. The valve only allowed air through after a preset time [1].

Off  Delay  Timer  (Normally  Open  Time  Delay  Valve):  An  Off  Delay  Timer  delayed  the
retraction of an actuator after the signal was removed. A normally open (NO) time delay valve was
used, which closed after a set time, allowing the actuator to remain extended momentarily [1].

A+B+A−B− Sequencing: This sequencing involved extending cylinder A, followed by cylinder
B. Then cylinder A retracted, followed by the retraction of cylinder B. It was achieved using roller
lever valves and logical sequencing.

A+A−B+B−  Sequencing:  This  sequence  required  cylinder  A  to  extend  and  retract  first,  then
cylinder B to extend and retract. This ensured the complete action of one actuator before the other
started.

Counter Operations: Counters were used to control the number of cycles an actuator performed.
When the pre-set count was reached, the operation was halted automatically.

Operations

Latching Circuit: A latching mechanism was simulated using a 5/2 Directional Control Valve
(DCV). A push button was used to energize the valve, initiating the operation. The output was
latched using a self-holding path, ensuring that once the button was released, the actuator remained
extended. This self-holding feature allowed the actuator to stay in the extended position without
continuous input, effectively demonstrating a latching circuit in the pneumatic system [2].

Fig.1. Latching Operation

On Delay Timer Circuit (Normally Closed): In this setup, a Normally Closed (NC) time delay
valve was connected before the Directional Control Valve (DCV) that controls the cylinder. When
the  push  button  was  pressed,  air  flowed  to  the  timer.  After  a  preset  delay,  the  timer  opened,
allowing air to reach the DCV and extending the cylinder. This circuit demonstrated the working
principle of an On Delay Timer, where the output action (cylinder extension) occurs only after the

specified delay time.

Fig.2. ON Delay Timer Operation

Off Delay Timer Circuit (Normally Open): In this configuration, a Normally Open (NO) time
delay  valve  was  used.  When  the  button  was  pressed,  the  air  flowed  through  the  timer.  Upon
releasing the button, the air continued to flow momentarily through the timer before being cut off.
This allowed the actuator to remain extended for a short duration after the button was released,
demonstrating  the  working  principle  of  an  Off  Delay  Timer.  The  actuator’s  extension  was
maintained for a brief period, even after the input signal was removed, showcasing the delay in
output.

Fig.3. OFF Delay Timer Operation

A+B+A−B− Logic Sequencing: In this operation, two double-acting cylinders (A and B) were
used to demonstrate the A+B+A−B− logic sequencing. Roller lever valves, positioned at the ends
of the cylinders, were employed to detect the positions of the cylinders. Proper sequencing was
achieved  using  signal  lines  and  logic  control,  ensuring  the  correct  order  of  operations.  The
sequence  followed  was  A+  (cylinder  A  extended),  B+  (cylinder  B  extended),  A−  (cylinder  A
retracted), and B− (cylinder B retracted), successfully completing the logic sequence as intended.

This demonstrated the integration of logic control in pneumatic systems for coordinated motion.

Fig.4. A+B+A−B− Logic Sequencing Operation

A+A−B+B− Logic Sequencing: In this operation, Cylinder A completed a full cycle (extension
and retraction) before Cylinder B started its cycle. Signal dependency was implemented using limit
valves to ensure that Cylinder B's sequence would only begin once Cylinder A had fully returned
to  its  initial  position.  This  sequencing  logic  prevented  both  cylinders  from  operating
simultaneously  and  ensured  that  Cylinder  B’s  motion  was  triggered  only  after  Cylinder  A  had
retracted, demonstrating controlled and dependent sequencing in pneumatic systems [2].

Fig.5. A+A−B+B− Logic Sequencing

Counter Operation: In this setup, a pneumatic counter was incorporated into the circuit to track
the  movement  of  the  cylinder.  The  counter  monitored  the  number  of  full  cycles  the  cylinder
completed, incrementing with each cycle. Once the preset number of operations was reached, the
actuator stopped automatically. This demonstrated the use of a pneumatic counter to control the
number  of  cycles  in  a  pneumatic  system,  ensuring  that  the  actuator  ceased  movement  after
completing the desired number of operations.

Discussion & Conclusion

Fig.6. Counter Operation

The circuits were successfully simulated in FluidSIM, and each operation was thoroughly verified.
The latching circuit effectively demonstrated the memory function in pneumatic systems, allowing
the actuator to remain in its last position without continuous input. The On and Off Delay timers
were crucial in providing necessary time delays, ensuring safety and enhancing process accuracy
in the system. The logic sequencing circuits (A+B+A−B− and A+A−B+B−) ensured structured
and coordinated operations of multiple actuators, highlighting their widespread use in industrial
automation for maintaining precise control over movements.

The counter circuits demonstrated their application in batch operations, where repetitive tasks need
to  be limited to  a set  number of cycles. The integration of sensors, such  as roller lever valves,
enabled  real-time  feedback  and  facilitated  logic-based  decision-making  for  accurate  control.
Overall, the operations underscored the critical role of timing, sequencing, and feedback in the
design and functioning of automated pneumatic systems, reinforcing their significance in modern
industrial applications.
